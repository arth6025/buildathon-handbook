The missing half

You'd never run two ads. So why run one page?

Your team runs dozens of creatives. Hundreds, in a good month. You kill the losers in a day and scale the winners by Friday. Nobody would accept running two ads all quarter and calling it a test.

Then every one of those clicks lands on the same page. The hook that earned the click, the angle that did the work, the promise that got made — none of it survives the transition. The page was written once, for nobody in particular.

It stayed that way for two reasons worth naming. Every landing page A/B test had to wait for statistical significance — two variants, three weeks, one answer. And every page had to be built by a person, which caps you at however many pages your team can hand-build before the season starts.

Both constraints are what a workflow removes.

Why a workflow

A person makes a page. A workflow makes the portfolio.

→
It doesn't stop

An agency ships a batch and invoices. A workflow is still producing at 2am on the day of the sale, when the winning angle changes and you need six more pages against it.

It compounds

Every page it produces teaches it what your customers respond to. A project starts from zero each time; a workflow starts from everything it has already made.

→
You own it

It's yours after the season, running against your store, with nobody to re-brief and no rate card between you and the next page.

This is the actual difference between buying creative and building capacity. The festive constraint isn't taste — it's throughput, and throughput is what a workflow is for.

The practical details

Questions you're about to ask.

Do we have to rebuild our store?

No. These are campaign landing pages that sit alongside your existing Shopify store and take paid traffic. Your theme, catalogue and checkout stay exactly as they are.

Do we need engineers to build the workflow?

No. The handbook is written for whoever currently briefs the creative — the prompt chain and brief templates are the build. If you want it wired deeper into your stack, that's what the forward-deployed engineer is for.

Does everything have to be built on ShopOS?

No. Build variants on ShopOS workflow, or bring pages your team already made with whatever tools you use. The matching works either way.

How do we know it's working?

Measured against a live holdout on your own traffic, so the number comes from your store rather than a benchmark. The rubric in the handbook is the same one we'd use.

Who is this for?

Brands on Shopify with a festive calendar already committed and media budget already allocated — where the constraint is creative supply and page throughput rather than strategy.
