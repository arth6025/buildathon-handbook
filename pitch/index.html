The missing half

You'd never run two ads. So why run one page?

Your team runs dozens of creatives. Hundreds, in a good month. You kill the losers in a day and scale the winners by Friday. Nobody would accept running two ads all quarter and calling it a test.

Then every one of those clicks lands on the same page. The hook that earned the click, the angle that did the work, the promise that got made — none of it survives the transition. The page was written once, for nobody in particular.

It stayed that way for a reason worth naming: every landing page A/B test had to wait for statistical significance. Two variants, three weeks, one answer. At that speed you can't build a portfolio, so nobody built one — and the post-click half of the funnel never got the treatment the pre-click half gets every day.

That constraint is what's changed. Pages can now be produced fast enough to be treated the way creative already is: many, cheap, judged on outcome.

The practical details

Questions you're about to ask.

Do we have to rebuild our store?

No. These are campaign landing pages that sit alongside your existing Shopify store and take paid traffic. Your theme, catalogue and checkout stay exactly as they are.

Do the pages have to be built on ShopOS?

No. Generate variants on ShopOS workflow, or bring pages your team already built with whatever tools you use. The matching works either way.

How long before we see anything?

The working session is thirty minutes and you leave it with a plan. The proof phase is scoped to fit inside your festive calendar — that constraint is the point, not an afterthought.

What if the lift isn't there?

You'll know, because it's measured against a live holdout on your own traffic rather than reported from a benchmark. There's no platform fee during the proof phase, and you keep the pages.

Who is this for?

Brands on Shopify with a festive calendar already committed and media budget already allocated — where the constraint is creative supply and page throughput rather than strategy.
